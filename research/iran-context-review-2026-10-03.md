# Iranian context and Persian usefulness review, 2026-10-03

Retrieval date: **2026-10-03**, Asia/Tehran. Background primary-source research for improving the existing five skills. Read the previous source review and current market/copy references first. This report evaluates coverage and evidence boundaries; it contains no new customer interviews, conversion experiment, national preference survey, or legal certification. Recommendations below are research/design inferences, distinguished from source observations.

## Finding

The existing skills have a sound evidence and scope foundation. Their remaining practical gap is local decision detail: whose Persian, which destination and service terms, which calendar and transaction unit, and which actual purchase state. More cultural claims would not fix that gap. Useful improvements should help agents discover the target context and handle concrete local tasks without turning one publisher's policy or one audience's voice into an Iranian universal.

## Access gaps and stale-page hazards

- [Academy orthography PDF](https://apll.ir/wp-content/uploads/2023/07/Dastour-e-Khat-17.04.1402.pdf) and [Enamad portal](https://enamad.ir/) produced internal tool errors with no readable content or specific HTTP status. Detailed Academy rules and current badge requirements remain unverified here. Do not substitute secondary commentary as a verified official rule.
- [Divar help](https://help.divar.ir/) and [Zibal docs](https://docs.zibal.ir/) were inaccessible in this research tool. No policy or API claim was inferred.
- [University of Tehran calendar portal](https://calendar.ut.ac.ir/) returned only a transfer shell. No year-specific holiday calendar was verified. Keep exact dates pending a readable dated publication.
- [Digikala contact FAQ](https://www.digikala.com/faq/question/80/) was readable but contains a “current year” holiday list without an identified year, alongside blanket availability language. This is a concrete example of why a recently retrieved first-party page can still contain ambiguous or stale claims. Resolve the applicable year/service terms before copying availability promises.

These access failures describe this environment; they do not establish global website downtime or absence of the documents. Linked sources retain their own rights. This review paraphrases selected relevant observations and does not bundle their manuals or merchant policies.

## Recommended changes to the five skills

1. **iran-market-discovery:** Add a short context brief distinguishing market territory from language/locale, serviceable destination from nationality, and observed behavior from assumed cultural preference. Recruit around the actual job, ability to use the service, device/access, purchase responsibility and relevant city/season. Ask for a recent concrete purchase and its alternative, obstacle and outcome; avoid leading questions such as “Iranians distrust online payment, don't they?” A Nowruz campaign is a test hypothesis until segment-specific evidence exists. Record the particular year's date and fulfillment implications separately from demand.
2. **iran-product-benchmark:** For relevant commerce journeys, compare total payable amount and unit, eligibility, destination/serviceability, delivery qualification, payment verification/pending/failure and return-request/refund states. Separate a policy declaration from a screenshot observation and completed journey. A logo's visibility does not verify issuer status or fulfillment quality. Never infer that a famous merchant's checkout conditions are national norms.
3. **persian-rtl-ux:** Make language, region, calendar, timezone, displayed unit and backend unit separate contract fields. Add numeric-field tests for Persian, Arabic-Indic and Latin input according to the declared contract, preserving leading-zero identifiers and original names. Test selected fonts for Persian glyphs, mixed strings, line-height/clipping and zoom. Add calendar boundary and transaction-unit mismatch cases without implementing unverified gateway logic.
4. **persian-brand-copy:** Add task-state microcopy examples for an unconfirmed payment, OTP wait/retry, an estimate versus booking, return requested versus approved, and qualified delivery. Choose formality and تو/شما from relationship/brand evidence; ask for user preference when addressing people. Holidays, humor, regional identity and familiarity should follow the brief, not presumed national taste. These examples must be labeled original and untested.
5. **persian-seo:** Make local intent depend on actual service territory and supported offer, not a city-name template. Preserve observed query spellings and Latin/Persian brand forms; test normalization instead of claiming engine equivalence. Seasonal content needs the correct year, service availability, updated facts and useful original content. Demand conclusions still require appropriate evidence; neither calendar salience nor a Trends spike proves purchase intent.

## Practical validation boundary

Blind task scenarios can test whether agents preserve these contracts, produce appropriate Persian copy and avoid invented promises. Browser tests can check rendering and input behavior. Neither proves usefulness to real customers. To substantiate that stronger claim, recruit actual intended users with consent, measure whether they understand the real next action/price/status and can complete the task, and record sample/context/limitations. Prefer comprehension and task outcomes over asking whether a design “feels Iranian.”
