# Iran Market Discovery

Turn a product question into an evidence-backed next decision. Apply the bundled [evidence policy](references/evidence-policy.md); national statistics provide context, not product-level demand.

For Iranian projects, read the **Iranian-context discovery questions** section in [the task reference](references/public-sources.md) when money, payments, phone/SMS, fulfillment or local dates affect the request. Apply relevant checks only; keep other Persian regions and the user’s scope intact.

For articles or content interfaces, read the editorial section of [the task reference](references/public-sources.md) when the request concerns local cultural fit, article comparison or reading/card usability. Apply only the relevant checks.

For customer interviews, switching decisions, willingness to pay or choosing a validation experiment, read [discovery methods](references/discovery-methods.md). It provides episode-based questions, assumption prioritization and evidence limits; choose the relevant method rather than requiring every framework.

## Guide the requester's decisions

- Anchor the conversation to the requested decision. Reuse the brief, previous answers and authorized evidence before asking for more information. Ask a clarification only when its answer could materially change the recommendation, test choice or feasibility; infer low-impact details as labeled assumptions.
- With **every clarifying question to the requester**, include your provisional recommendation or a conditional default and a short reason grounded in available evidence. Make the choice easy to assess, without presenting a preference as a fact or implying the user has accepted it. For an unknown factual answer, recommend how to establish it rather than inventing the answer. Follow an explicit user preference for a different question format.
- Respect one-question-at-a-time requests: one decision-relevant question with its recommendation is enough for that turn. This controls pacing, not a requirement to keep asking questions. Do not bundle several independent decisions into one question.
- Treat "I don't know", "all", "it varies" and a skipped question as usable information. Preserve unresolved distinctions; ask a targeted follow-up only if that distinction blocks the decision. Otherwise carry the gap into the recommendation and next test instead of rephrasing the same question.
- Deliver a provisional result as soon as the decision, useful evidence/gaps and a feasible next investigation can be stated. Explain the recommendation, supporting evidence, what could change it and the next test; ask a further question only if it changes that result. If a critical blocker remains, give the supported portion and one focused question with a recommendation. There is no universal question-count limit.
- Finish discovery before expanding into execution details such as Instagram profile audits, lead-list building or message drafting, unless those are the requested decision or necessary feasibility checks. Put optional implementation work after the decision as a next step. Planning targets are proposed experiment criteria, not forecasts derived from budget alone.
- Keep requester decision support separate from **customer research interviews**. The requester benefits from your recommendation; interview participants should describe their own experiences without a suggested answer. Read the methods reference for neutral interviewing; attach any researcher recommendations separately, not inside the participant-facing question.

## Workflow

1. **Frame the decision.** Establish category, proposed customer/job, geography, period and decision to make using the dialogue rules above. Finish with an explicit decision statement and scope, not necessarily a question to the requester; record language separately from market/service area and use the source guide’s local-context interview prompts when segment assumptions matter.
2. **Collect evidence.** Read the [source guide](references/public-sources.md) when selecting Iranian sources. Prefer original publications, company terms and user-supplied research. Capture publication/collection dates, segment, units and limitations using the [evidence record](references/evidence-record.md). A homepage or unavailable report is not evidence for its numbers. For interviews, reconstruct an actual episode and separate participant reports, observed actions and researcher interpretation. Finish with an evidence ledger, including gaps and contradictory findings.
3. **Compare alternatives.** Include direct competitors and substitutes such as informal providers or doing nothing when relevant. Compare customer job, offer, published price/currency, access, fulfillment and visible trust conditions. Mark unknowns instead of assigning unsupported market share or quality scores. Finish with a task-relevant comparison linked to evidence.
4. **Make the decision.** Explain which option evidence supports, which inference connects it to the recommendation and what could overturn it. Prioritize unresolved assumptions by consequence if wrong and weakness of relevant evidence; explain the ordering without fabricated scores. If demand is unproven, recommend validation before commitment. Match confidence to evidence relevance and sampling, not source count.
5. **Design the next test.** Choose a method that tests the prioritized uncertainty, with participants, observable signal, time/cost bound, owner and decision after success, failure or inconclusive evidence. Agree quantitative thresholds with the owner when meaningful; otherwise specify observable qualitative criteria. Label unagreed criteria proposed. Distinguish stated interest, clicks, qualified requests, paid orders and repeat use. Finish with a practical validation plan within the user's authorized scope; planning research does not authorize recruiting, publishing, spending or collecting payments.

## Deliverable and completion

Return decision/scope, evidence ledger, task-relevant alternative or segment comparison, prioritized unresolved assumptions, recommendation with confidence, and next tests. Separate what is known, inferred and still unknown. Every material factual claim needs a source or user-evidence record; every demand claim must distinguish observation from hypothesis. State units explicitly, including rial versus toman and nominal versus inflation-adjusted figures when applicable.

With no browsing or dated sources, deliver a research plan and labeled hypotheses, not a current-market conclusion. With inaccessible sources, show the supported result and missing checks. Completion means the user can decide what to investigate or test next; it does not mean demand has been proved.
