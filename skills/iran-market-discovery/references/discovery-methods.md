# Customer discovery and validation methods

Use the section matching the decision. These are repository-authored operational adaptations of the sources below, not verbatim book extracts or a universally validated sequence. Apply the [evidence policy](evidence-policy.md) and relevant [Iranian-context questions](public-sources.md). A research framework guides inquiry; it does not establish Iranian customer behavior.

## 1. Episode-based interviews

Use when the customer problem, present workaround or purchase process is unclear. The Mom Test's official description addresses biased feedback; the detailed interview procedure here is an authored adaptation, not a claim to have read the full book.

- Recruit people who recently experienced the job, including people who abandoned it or used a substitute. Choose recency to fit the buying cycle and recall quality; explain recruitment bias and missing perspectives.
- Start with a concrete episode: trigger, sequence, alternatives considered, actual choice, cost/time, outcome and unresolved difficulty. Identify user, decision maker and payer separately where relevant.
- Ask neutrally before showing the proposed solution. For a distinct prototype or offer test, explain the transition and record responses separately. There is no fixed interview/pitch ratio or mandatory sample size.
- Recommendations accompanying clarifications to the project owner are decision support, not a format for customer interviews. Keep participant-facing prompts neutral; place your advice about recruitment, interpretation or next actions in separate researcher notes. When the owner is also a participant, distinguish their recalled experience from a product preference or planning decision.
- Keep anonymized participant statements separate from directly observed actions and your interpretation. Retain counterexamples. End with the uncertainty reduced and what remains unknown.

Authored Persian prompts:

- «آخرین بار که تعمیرکار لازم داشتی، از کجا شروع کردی؟»
- «چه گزینه هایی داشتی و در نهایت کدوم رو انتخاب کردی؟»
- «چقدر پرداخت کردی و هزینه نهایی رو کی فهمیدی؟»
- «کاری بود که نتونستی انجام بدی یا منصرف شدی؟ چه اتفاقی افتاد؟»

An answer to “would you buy?” records stated intention, not a purchase forecast. Compliments can inform perception; they do not establish demand. Counts from a convenience sample describe that sample only.

## 2. Jobs and switching decisions

Use when deciding which customer/job to serve or why someone might change their existing approach. The Christensen Institute describes choices through circumstances and functional, social and emotional progress.

Reconstruct the trigger, current approach, desired progress, alternatives and decision timeline. Ask what made the current approach inadequate, what attracted the alternative, and what concerns or familiar routines held the person back. Treat these as qualitative explanations linked to episodes, not quantities in a forces equation.

Return a bounded job statement plus supporting episodes, contrary evidence and switching uncertainties. Manual work, a referral, a phone call or doing nothing can be alternatives even without a direct software competitor. New-category ideas still need evidence about the underlying job; missing purchase history is a limitation, not proof of no need.

## 3. Assumptions and solution choices

Use when several uncertainties compete for effort. Strategyzer describes prioritizing assumptions and matching experiments to them. Product Talk connects desired outcomes, customer opportunities, possible solutions and assumption tests.

Keep a compact list: **assumption | consequence if false | evidence IDs/gap | priority reason | next test**. Consider customer desirability, ability to deliver, economic viability and external dependencies only where relevant. High consequence plus weak relevant evidence is a reason to test earlier; time, cost and dependencies may change the order. Numerical scoring is optional and needs an explicit basis.

Where solution choice is the decision, connect the desired outcome to an evidenced customer need and compare plausible solutions. Mark an unevidenced need as a hypothesis. This adaptation uses no universal solution count; the cited Product Talk article recommends three as its own practice. A feature request is input, not proof of the underlying need.

An MVP is one possible learning vehicle. Choose a focused assumption test, prototype, manual/concierge service or MVP according to uncertainty, cost, feasibility and required evidence. Use existing product data when available; discovery need not always restart with interviews.

## 4. Willingness to pay and observed commitment

Use when deciding whether or how to test an offer/price. This evidence distinction is repository guidance, not a universal scale or a numerical correction model.

| Signal | What it can support | What remains unknown |
|---|---|---|
| Compliment or stated price | Perception or a stated preference in that interview context | Whether the person will buy this offer |
| Past spending on an alternative | Reported behavior and budget context; corroborate if possible | Whether they will switch or pay the proposed price |
| Offer/ad click | Interest in the displayed message under those conditions | Purchase, affordability and fulfillment |
| Qualified request or booked pilot | A specific next action; describe who qualified and what was agreed | Completed payment, delivery and retention |
| Paid order or deposit | A transaction under stated terms; retain cancellation/refund status | Repeat demand, profitable service and broader adoption |
| Repeat paid use | Observed recurrence within the measured cohort | Population demand or sustainable economics beyond it |

Keep offered price, actual paid amount, rial/toman, date, fees, eligibility, channel and denominator explicit. For a funnel, preserve each stage and tracking gaps. Test ranges may be informed by prior spending and alternatives; do not divide stated willingness to pay by a fixed coefficient. A missing or zero payment count needs diagnosis, not an automatic verdict that demand is absent.

## 5. Choose the next experiment

Adapt Strategyzer's hypothesis/test/metric/criterion structure using the [validation record](evidence-record.md). Select the least costly feasible method capable of reducing the target uncertainty, with sufficient evidence for the decision's risk:

| Uncertainty | Candidate method | Evidence boundary |
|---|---|---|
| Problem/process unclear | Episode interview or consented observation | Explanatory evidence, not prevalence |
| Offer/instructions misunderstood | Prototype comprehension/task test | Understanding/usability, not paid demand |
| Message or channel interest | Transparently described offer/landing test | Exposure, click or request; not a sale |
| Delivery workflow uncertain | Authorized manual/concierge pilot | Limited operational feasibility, not scaled margin |
| Price and purchase uncertain | Clearly disclosed priced offer or paid pilot | Actual commitment only to the observed stage |

Before execution, specify participant criteria, recruitment/channel, hypothesis, procedure, metric or qualitative observation, time/spend limits, owner, proposed/agreed criteria and actions for success, failure and inconclusive results. A qualitative criterion can concern whether people understand what happens after a request; a quantitative threshold needs a rationale and a defined denominator. Keep the original criterion when reviewing results; explain any prospective revision.

Plan first. Research requests alone do not authorize contacting people, launching ads, publishing pages, taking deposits or running transactions. Before authorized execution, disclose product availability, price and applicable delivery/refund terms; use consent and minimal data collection. Do not offer unavailable services as if ready. Payment evidence is useful only when collecting it is appropriate and authorized.
