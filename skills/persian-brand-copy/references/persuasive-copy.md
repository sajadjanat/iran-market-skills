# Persuasive copy strategy

Use this reference for a campaign, sales page, offer, launch or a set of related messages when the user wants help with the persuasion strategy as well as the wording. The existing brief, evidence, Persian-writing and scope rules still apply.

## Plan the argument

For substantial persuasion work, sketch only the decisions needed for the requested placement:

1. **People:** Who is the stated audience, what situation brings them here, what outcome do they want, and what friction or concern matters? Use direct customer language only when it is supplied or gathered with permission. Keep a quote tied to its source and context; one person's words do not establish a segment-wide belief.
2. **Positioning:** Why might this audience choose this offer over an alternative? State a concrete, supportable distinction. If the distinction or alternative is unknown, label it as a question or hypothesis instead of claiming the brand is unique, best or first.
3. **Promise:** Name the practical result for this audience and the conditions that bound it. Trace a feature through its practical benefit to the meaningful outcome (“so what?”); include only steps and results the product can actually support. Do not turn a benefit into a guarantee.
4. **Proof:** Match each material claim to evidence the user supplied or an accessible source. Record what it supports, its scope and any required qualification. Product demonstrations, dated metrics and customer stories can each support different claims; use testimonials, logos and results only with provenance and permission. If proof is missing, narrow the claim or name the evidence needed.
5. **Priority:** Explain why acting now matters only when there is a real, verified deadline, capacity limit or consequence of delay. If the cost of waiting is unknown, do not invent fear, scarcity or urgency; give the reader a clear next step without a pressure tactic.
6. **Process:** Explain the actual steps or mechanism that lead toward the promised result. A “common belief / why it falls short / alternative approach” structure is useful only when the belief and limitation are evidenced and the offered process is real. Do not invent a proprietary method.

For each draft, the six questions are a planning aid, not six mandatory sections. Mark assumptions and unresolved proof; ask only for missing information that changes a material claim or the requested decision. For brief copy, use only the relevant questions.

## Shape the writing

Use these five checks during revision:

- **Clear:** A reader can understand the point and next action on a first read.
- **Concise:** Each phrase advances the message; remove repetition without losing a needed qualification.
- **Concrete:** Prefer a supplied, specific outcome or example to an empty superlative.
- **Conversational:** Use the audience's demonstrated vocabulary and the approved register; do not infer one voice for all Persian speakers.
- **Cadenced:** Vary sentence length where it helps the text flow when read aloud. Keep clarity and accessibility ahead of rhythm.

Choose structures by placement, not habit:

- **Hook:** Try distinct identity, outcome or promise angles only when each accurately fits the audience and offer. Review benefit, relevance, credibility and perceived effort as qualitative questions; this is not a score, virality formula or performance forecast.
- **Body:** `What / Why / How` can organize an explanation. `But / Therefore` can carry a real change in a story. Do not force either structure onto every placement.
- **CTA:** Check who should act, what one action they take, when it is actually available and how the control or route works. Match the words to the real destination and state; an imagined deadline is not a timing instruction.

## Offer value and journey

When useful, identify whether the offer helps the stated audience save time, earn or retain money, or reduce a specific risk. Treat these as candidate value angles: use only the ones supported by the brief and relevant to the audience. Note whether the placement supports discovery, consideration or action so its proof and CTA fit the reader's next decision. Do not force a funnel onto editorial or interface copy.

## Completion check

A persuasive draft is ready for review when its intended audience and action are clear, its central promise and positioning fit the supplied offer, material claims have support or visible qualifications, urgency is factual, and the CTA reaches the stated next step. Audience response and conversion remain hypotheses until tested with the intended audience; do not promise a lift.
