# Choosing what to publish or improve

Use when deciding between revising existing articles, publishing new ideas or adding a product tutorial. Keep the user's authorized editorial choice separate from an evidence-based growth recommendation.

## Read the available evidence first

Inventory project content, analytics exports and authorized connectors. Use existing access before requesting browser access, credentials or another export. Name the data source and coverage. A project dashboard may contain traffic, acquisition, events or feedback that a public page cannot reveal. Do not substitute page inspection for those metrics.

Separate search impressions/clicks, page visits, engagement, task completion and actual qualitative feedback. Low traffic is not proof of poor prose; a scroll event is not proof of satisfaction. If asked whether performance improved, compare equivalent windows with dates, sample sizes, definitions and tracking/deployment changes. Report missing channels or outcomes instead of treating them as zero.

## Choose by the reader's job

| Evidence or brief | Candidate action | What remains to verify |
|---|---|---|
| Existing page attracts relevant queries but misses their answer | Improve that page's useful answer/title/structure | Query fit and page-level outcomes after change |
| Users repeatedly fail a real app task | Publish a verified tutorial | Flow accuracy and whether readers complete the task |
| Distinct recurring question lacks a useful page | Propose a new article | Original value, sources and overlap with existing URLs |
| Owner explicitly requests an idea article without demand data | Fulfill that brief, labeling growth assumptions | Search demand and readership response |
| Only sparse or incomparable performance data exists | Give a bounded editorial hypothesis and measurement plan | Comparable evidence before claiming improvement |

Do not make insufficient search data a blanket reason to stop useful authorized publishing. Do not invent a content calendar or numeric benefit merely to fill an audit.

For every proposed article, state the reader/situation, one useful outcome, evidence for the topic, existing-page overlap and what makes the new piece worth reading. Choose fewer proposals if the evidence cannot support the requested count; explain the gap or offer explicitly exploratory options. Keep the supplied register in titles; search optimization does not require formalizing a conversational brand.

## Search and local fit

When Trends is requested, record query/topic choice, geography, time range, category, search type, comparison setup and access date. Relative scores from differently normalized exports are not directly comparable absolute volumes. If access fails, name the limitation; do not present a search-results page as Trends data.

Iranian search results and local publications can reveal terminology and subjects. They do not establish population popularity. Compare what those pages actually explain, identify an unanswered reader question, and create original examples instead of paraphrasing a competitor's article. Prefer primary instructions for factual game rules or product behavior.

## Delivery and follow-up

Separate observed problems, proposed editorial changes and later measured outcomes. Preserve intended category, canonical article URL and internal links when editing, and investigate redirects only when URLs change. Do not promise indexing or traffic gains. Technical verification can finish while business impact remains unmeasured.
